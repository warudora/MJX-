MTR Japan Nexus — General Server Regulations

Enacted October 5, 2026

Preamble

These Regulations establish common rules for using services operated by MTR Japan Nexus (hereinafter “MJX”).

In these Regulations, “the Services” means the Discord server, Minecraft server, and other related services operated by MJX.

Additional rules established separately for each service also apply.

Article 1 — Legal Compliance

Users must comply with Japanese law and the laws applicable in the areas where they reside.

Article 2 — Language

1. Japanese and English are the primary languages used in the Services.

2. Users must make reasonable efforts to communicate effectively with one another.

3. Management may designate the language to be used in particular services or channels when necessary.

Article 3 — Prohibition of Harassment and Nuisance

Users must not engage in any of the following conduct:

1. Harassing other users, intimidating conduct, or excessive provocation

2. Defamation, abusive language, insults, and discriminatory statements

3. Acts that unfairly disparage other users or organizations

4. Political or religious statements, discussions, solicitation, or other conduct that management deems inappropriate

5. Posting material contrary to public order or morals, adult-oriented content, or excessively sexual content

6. Interfering with other users' activities

7. Other conduct that management deems harassing, disruptive, or inappropriate

Even if the person had no malicious intent, disciplinary measures may be imposed depending on the nature and effects of the conduct.

Article 4 — Handling of Personal Information

1. Users must not publish another user’s personal information without that person’s permission.

2. Users should also exercise great care when disclosing their own personal information.

3. Collecting, publishing, spreading, or otherwise handling personal information in ways management deems inappropriate may result in disciplinary action.

Article 5 — Prohibition of Spam and Dangerous Content

1. Spam, excessive repeated posting, and mass posting of meaningless messages are prohibited.

2. Posting viruses, malware, or files and links that may harm users or the services is prohibited.

3. Posting links or content equivalent to R15 or R18 material is prohibited. MJX management may delete content or impose measures when it considers the material dangerous or inappropriate, regardless of the destination linked.

Article 6 — Prohibition of Unauthorized Advertising and Solicitation

Users must not engage in commercial advertising, promotion, solicitation to join other servers, or similar activities without management's permission.

Article 7 — Handling of Related Organizations and Other Servers

1. Discussions concerning the MTR Global Server Alliance (MGSA), other affiliated organizations, and other Minecraft servers should generally take place in channels or spaces designated by MJX management.

2. As a rule, MJX does not get involved in disputes that arise on other servers. This does not apply to matters involving MGSA.

3. When management judges that activities involving MGSA significantly affect MJX operations, those activities may be subject to measures even if they occurred outside MJX.

4. When necessary, MJX may share information about users’ violations and similar matters with MGSA and other related organizations.

5. If an MGSA-wide ban on a specific user is formally adopted through MGSA's collective decision-making process, all MGSA member servers, including MJX, must implement the decision by suspending the user's access or taking other required measures.

6. Measures under the preceding paragraph are based on the MGSA-wide ban decision; individual member servers do not independently decide whether that decision applies.

7. Management may consider information about disciplinary actions by MGSA or related organizations when necessary. Except for MGSA-wide bans, such information alone does not automatically result in disciplinary action within MJX.

Article 8 — Use of Multiple Accounts

1. Users may access the Services through multiple accounts, including secondary accounts.

2. Harassment, evasion of disciplinary action, and other inappropriate acts using multiple accounts are prohibited.

3. If management determines that multiple accounts are being used inappropriately, it may impose restrictions on their use or take other necessary measures.

Article 9 — Other Inappropriate Conduct

Even if conduct is not explicitly listed in these Regulations, management may take necessary measures if it determines that the conduct disrupts MJX operations, interactions among users, or activities with related organizations.

Article 10 — Application of These Rules to Administrators

1. As a general rule, MJX administrators must also comply with these Regulations.

2. However, an administrator may act contrary to these Regulations with the approval of the Supreme Administrator.

3. At their own discretion, the Supreme Administrator may waive the application of all or part of these Regulations or take actions that would otherwise conflict with them.

Article 11 — Response to Violations and Disciplinary Measures

1. If a user violates these Regulations, management may impose the following measures according to the nature and severity of the violation:

• Caution • Warning • Timeout • Temporary suspension of access • Permanent suspension of access

2. Management determines the type and duration of disciplinary measures in light of the violation’s nature, circumstances, and effects.

3. Management may combine multiple measures when necessary.

4. Disciplinary measures may be imposed without notifying the offender in advance.

5. Measures following a formally decided MGSA-wide ban are governed by Article 7.

Article 12 — Appeals

1. Users may request an explanation of disciplinary measures from management.

2. As a rule, filing an appeal does not suspend the effect of a disciplinary measure.

3. Management may amend or withdraw a disciplinary measure after reviewing an appeal if it considers doing so necessary.

4. MJX cannot independently change or revoke measures based on an MGSA-wide ban decision.

Article 13 — Automated Detection Systems and Related Matters

1. The Services may use automated detection systems such as Wick.

2. Users may request management to review disciplinary measures resulting from automated detection.

3. If a false detection is confirmed, management will modify or withdraw the measure as necessary.

4. Restrictions may be imposed depending on the circumstances, even when account hijacking or other acts occurred without the user’s intent.

Article 14 — Reviewing the Rules

When using the Services, users must review and comply with these Regulations and any other rules established by management.

Article 15 — Revisions to the Regulations

1. Management may revise these Regulations when necessary.

2. Revisions may be made without advance notice.

3. Revised Regulations take effect upon publication.

Article 16 — Rules for Individual Services

1. Minecraft servers and other individual services are subject to their own separately established regulations in addition to these Regulations.

2. Where service-specific regulations differ from these Regulations, management determines which rules apply according to the service or conduct concerned.

3. Users must comply with both these Regulations and the rules applicable to each individual service.

Supplementary Provisions

1. These Regulations took effect on November 1, 2026.

2. Management decides matters not covered by these Regulations when necessary.

3. Management decides any questions concerning the interpretation of these Regulations.

MTR Japan Nexus — Builder Regulations

Established October 26, 2026

MTR Japan Nexus

Preamble

These Regulations establish the qualifications, authority, duties, and administrative procedures of Builders in MTR Japan Nexus (MJX).

A Builder is a person who performs construction, urban development, or development and upkeep of railway and transportation infrastructure within MJX.

MJX respects Builders' freedom to develop while protecting participants' rights, existing facilities, the server environment, and the economic system.

These Regulations apply together with the MJX General Server Regulations and other related regulations.

Chapter 1 — General Provisions

Article 1 — Purpose

1. These Regulations aim to balance Builders' freedom to pursue ongoing development with the interests of MJX as a whole.

2. Builders must comply with these Regulations and related rules, and make reasonable efforts to contribute to MJX's development in cooperation with other participants.

3. These Regulations do not restrict Builders' development activities without legitimate grounds.

Article 2 — Builder Qualifications and Selection

1. Builder qualifications are obtained through the MTR Global Server Alliance (MGSA) selection process or procedures recognized by MJX.

2. Qualification categories, selection methods, and activity permissions are governed by MGSA's common framework and relevant MJX regulations.

3. If MJX introduces its own selection procedures or powers, it must separately specify their details and conditions of application. MJX must not modify MGSA’s system beyond its own authority.

4. A Builder may be subject to measures under Article 19 for violations committed even after obtaining their qualification.

Article 3 — Fundamental Builder Permissions

1. Builders may develop buildings, roads, railway lines, stations, transportation facilities, shops, and similar structures within MJX.

2. Builders are granted the permissions needed for development according to their qualification category.

3. As a rule, no advance application is required to acquire land, and free development is permitted. However, restrictions based on others’ rights and related regulations still apply.

4. Builders must not improperly obstruct another person’s development or modify, move, remove, or destroy existing buildings, equipment, or property without permission.

5. Management may restrict development to a reasonable and necessary extent to ensure safety, meet public needs, or protect the rights of others.

Article 4 — Duties During Activities

Builders must comply with the following requirements:

1. Respect other participants' buildings, property, facilities, and development activities.

2. Make reasonable efforts not to place an excessive load on the server.

3. Cooperate to the extent reasonably necessary when management requests reasonable assistance with investigating violations, modifying equipment, or reducing system load.

4. If problems arise from their activities, Builders must promptly assess the situation and report it to management when necessary.

Article 5 — Relationship with MJX Management

1. Routine decisions concerning Builders are made by General Administrators or other authorized management personnel.

2. Each administrator's authority is governed by MJX's administrator regulations and the allocation of responsibilities.

3. Final decisions concerning MJX as a whole are governed by the Supreme Administrator Regulations.

4. Management must make decisions in light of applicable regulations, the facts, fairness, and their impact on MJX as a whole.

5. As a rule, management explains the reasons for measures that affect a Builder’s rights or activities.

Chapter 2 — Development and rights management

Article 6 — Priority Holding Rights

1. With management's approval, a Builder may obtain priority holding rights over designated areas, buildings, railway lines, or similar assets.

2. Priority holding rights are intended to coordinate development among Builders. They do not constitute unlimited land ownership or blanket authority to prohibit legitimate development by others.

3. Applications for priority holding rights must identify the area covered, the person responsible for managing it, and the development purpose. MJX management records and administers approved rights.

4. Management will separately establish and announce specific criteria, including permitted areas and numbers of holdings, application procedures, and approval methods.

5. Ordinary development that does not involve priority holding rights remains subject to the Article 3 principle that advance applications are generally unnecessary.

6. Where Builders' development plans conflict, the parties should first seek agreement through discussion. If they cannot resolve the matter, management will coordinate a solution considering existing rights, development status, and public interest.

7. Holders must make reasonable efforts to continue developing and managing their holdings. Progress includes construction, specific renovation, and substantive design-based work; a declaration of ownership or an announced plan alone normally does not count.

8. Rights generally remain in effect during an approved leave. However, where they seriously obstruct others’ development or public needs require action, changes or cancellation may be considered after notifying the holder and reviewing the circumstances.

9. As a rule, prior notice and an opportunity to explain or improve must be provided before changes or cancellation of rights are considered.

10. In cases of serious violations or emergency safety needs, management may temporarily restrict rights and must explain the reasons to the person concerned as soon as reasonably possible.

Article 7 — Development Areas and Others’ Works

1. Builders must respect areas, buildings, facilities, and transportation infrastructure developed or managed by others.

2. As a general rule, Builders must obtain the rights holder's permission before changing, moving, removing, or renovating someone else's work.

3. If the rights holder is unknown or cannot be contacted, consult management.

4. In an emergency involving safety or a serious disruption, management may authorize action only to the extent necessary.

5. Disputes concerning rights are governed by Article 20.

Article 8 — Identification of Owners

1. Builders must install signs identifying the owners or managers of buildings, railway lines, and facilities where necessary.

2. Signs must include information identifying the responsible party, such as the Builder's name or organization name.

3. For facilities of significant public importance or where the administrator's identity needs to be verified, management may require identification signs for stated, reasonable reasons.

4. Rights are not lost solely because an owner-identification sign is absent.

5. Where ownership is unclear, management may review records, the circumstances of installation, and the surrounding situation before deciding how to respond.

Article 9 — Registration and Sharing of Schematics

1. A Builder may register their own buildings, or buildings for which they hold the necessary rights to grant usage permission to others, as Schematics.

2. Registration is completed by posting the building’s name, completion photos, creator name, and other required details in the designated “Building List” on Discord or a similar platform. The saved filename should generally match the registered name.

3. The registrant must have the authority to permit installation, copying, modification, and use by other Builders within MJX.

4. A registered Schematic is deemed licensed for other Builders to use for urban development within MJX, within the conditions stated at registration.

5. As a rule, the permission in the preceding paragraph includes the following acts, and individual permission is not required for each installation.

• Installation and use within MJX • Installation at multiple sites • Changes to exteriors, interiors, and facilities to suit the surroundings or intended purpose

6. Any additional conditions or restrictions must be stated at registration so other Builders can check them before use. Conditions that conflict with these Regulations require management review.

7. Unregistered Schematics must not be used or installed without permission from their creator or legitimate rights holder.

8. Builders must not register another person's copyrighted work or any data they lack authority to register or license for reuse.

9. When a significantly modified building is re-registered as a separate building, the original creator must generally be credited. For legitimate reasons, alternative arrangements may be discussed with management. Misleading attribution is prohibited.

10. Creator attribution for ordinary installations or minor modifications is optional unless additional conditions were specified at registration.

11. Registrants may request deregistration through management and thereby stop new uses of the registered Schematic in the future.

12. As a rule, buildings placed before deregistration, and Schematics already acquired or placed, are not removed or reclaimed. However, new buildings generally may not be placed after deregistration, even if the user already possesses the data.

13. For development projects in progress when registration is withdrawn, management decides whether work may continue, taking progress and impacts on those concerned into account.

14. If rights infringement, serious violations of these Regulations, or similar problems are confirmed, management may take necessary measures, including suspension of use or removal.

15. The permissions granted under this Article do not confer rights that the registrant does not possess. They must not infringe legal rights or third-party rights.

Article 10 — Create Mod and Technical Equipment

1. Builders may use the Create Mod and similar tools according to MJX’s Mod environment and related regulations.

2. Technical equipment must not significantly disrupt server stability or the activities of other participants.

3. High-load equipment, including power-generation and transmission mechanisms, must comply with the prohibited and permitted equipment lists established by management.

4. The list must specify the covered blocks, mechanisms, and conditions of use, and may be revised to reflect Mod updates.

5. The adoption or amendment of a list must generally be announced in advance along with its effective date. Unannounced restrictions may not be applied retroactively, except for violations of existing rules or emergency safety measures.

6. If excessive load or malfunctions are confirmed, management may require the equipment concerned to be stopped, modified, or removed.

7. Management may impose temporary restrictions in emergencies but must explain the reasons as promptly as possible.

8. As a general rule, the MTR Mod must be used as the main system for railway construction.

Chapter 3 — Economic activities and prohibited matters

Article 11 — Distribution of Resources and Coins

1. Builders must not directly distribute unlimited quantities of items, construction materials, equipment, or other resources generated in Creative mode to Visitors or others.

2. To maintain MJX’s economic balance, Builders must not distribute unlimited coins directly or improperly bypass the economic system to distribute coins.

3. Legitimate payment for work, rewards officially recognized by management, event prizes, and materials needed for joint development are not prohibited across the board.

4. Exceptions in the preceding paragraph remain subject to the Economic Regulations and other related rules and do not permit unconditional distribution.

5. Activities with a major economic impact, such as supplying materials to new participants or distributing materials in bulk or continuously, are subject to approval criteria separately established by management.

6. If it is unclear whether distribution is permitted or what its limit is, check with management in advance.

7. If improper distribution or economic manipulation is discovered, management may take necessary measures, including recovering items and coins, and request distribution records and an explanation of the circumstances.

Article 12 — Store Operations and Contracting

1. A Builder may commission other participants to perform guidance, driving, filming, construction, shop-staff duties, or similar work, and may pay them in coins.

2. Business activities such as stores and services are subject to the Economic Regulations and other related rules. Official stores and businesses authorized by MJX management are recommended.

3. Advertising and solicitation are governed by Article 6 of the MJX General Server Regulations. Advertising that requires permission must receive prior approval.

4. Transactions involving real money or other value outside the game are subject to separately established rules. Any required approval must be obtained in advance.

5. As a rule, the parties must agree in advance on compensation, deadlines, scope of work, and other terms of outsourced tasks.

6. Disputes concerning outsourced work are governed by Article 20.

Article 13 — Confidential Information

1. Builders must not disclose nonpublic information learned through their activities to third parties without legitimate grounds.

2. Confidential information includes nonpublic personal data, login credentials, unpublished management decisions, and security-related information.

3. Confidential information may be disclosed only to the extent necessary where management has formally authorized disclosure, disclosure is needed to report or investigate a violation, or disclosure is required by law or legitimate official procedures.

4. Disclosures must be limited to the minimum necessary. Consult management when a decision is difficult.

Article 14 — Prohibitions

1. Builders must not violate these Regulations, the MJX General Server Regulations, or other related rules.

2. The following buildings, displays, and activities are prohibited:

• Buildings, displays, or activities that unreasonably obstruct another person's buildings, land, equipment, or activities • Buildings, displays, or activities that seriously interfere with the server or participants’ activities • Facilities or activities prohibited by the common rules • Buildings, displays, or activities that management deems markedly inconsistent with MJX’s operational purposes or building environment on specific grounds • Buildings, displays, or activities primarily intended to harass, threaten, insult, exclude, or attack particular users

3. In determining whether something is inappropriate, management must consider the work’s content, location, purpose, effects on the surroundings, and relevant regulations together.

4. A design must not be prohibited solely because it is unusual or does not match an administrator’s personal preferences.

5. To ensure consistent decisions, management records cases of approval and refusal, along with the criteria used, and publishes this information when necessary.

6. Restrictions on religious facilities must be consistent with the common and related regulations, and their scope and reasons must be stated. Any MJX-specific restriction must have its authority and basis confirmed.

7. Except in emergencies, management should verify the facts and seek explanations from the parties concerned wherever possible.

8. Mere differences of opinion or reasonable criticism of management must not, by themselves, be treated as violations.

Chapter 4 — Leave of Absence and Withdrawal

Article 15 — Review of Activity Status

1. Management may review Builders' activity status and assigned assets to maintain qualifications and permissions, arrange handovers, or resolve operational issues.

2. When requesting confirmation, management should specify the purpose and response deadline wherever possible.

3. A Builder’s qualification must not be revoked immediately solely because the Builder did not respond.

4. If contact cannot be established for an extended period and operational difficulties arise, management may respond according to Articles 16 and 17.

Article 16 — Leave of Absence

1. A Builder may apply to management for leave when there are reasonable grounds, stating the reason and anticipated duration where possible.

2. Builders are temporarily exempt from ordinary activity obligations during approved leave. Their permissions, priority holding rights, and assigned projects remain subject to these Regulations.

3. When management seeks confirmation about permissions or assigned projects during leave, it must generally allow at least seven days from the notice for a response. This period may be adjusted for reasonable circumstances, including emergencies.

4. If no response is received by the deadline, management must make reasonable efforts to contact the Builder again and verify the circumstances; it must not immediately cancel the leave or revoke their qualifications.

5. If neither substantive activity nor communication can be confirmed for at least 60 days, as a rule, management may review whether to maintain the Builder’s permissions and assigned projects.

6. The review must consider leave requests, advance notice, the person’s circumstances, and the status of projects. Where leave has been approved or reasonable circumstances exist, inactivity alone must not determine the outcome.

7. Even when regular contact is maintained, management may make necessary inquiries based on the status of managed assets and their impact on development.

8. Any limits on the number or duration of leaves or intervals between applications must be separately established and announced as necessary.

Article 17 — Long-Term Suspension of Railway Lines

1. As a general rule, if no substantive railway-line construction, maintenance, operation, or similar activity can be confirmed for at least 60 days, management may review the line's status and future plans.

2. A completed line that is operating and being managed normally must not be deemed inactive solely because no construction is underway.

3. Management must consider reasonable circumstances such as leave of absence, prior notification, or technical problems.

4. Management may request an explanation or plans from the owner, but must not immediately revoke rights or forcibly remove a line solely because activity has stopped.

5. If the owner is no longer an active Builder, management should consider continued management, transfer, or joint management in light of the owner’s circumstances, existing rights, the line’s public importance, and the need for handover.

6. If the parties cannot agree on a transferee, management may select one through a reasonable process, such as inviting applicants, coordinating candidates, or holding a lottery.

7. As a general rule, rights holders must receive advance notice and an opportunity to explain before their rights are amended or revoked.

8. In a safety or public emergency, necessary measures such as temporary changes of management may be taken.

Article 18 — Handling of withdrawal and registration data

1. A Builder who withdraws must notify management.

2. Permissions, assigned projects, priority holding rights, Schematics, resource packs, and similar matters must be handled in accordance with these Regulations and related procedures.

3. As a guideline, management proceeds with necessary checks and procedures within 14 days of notification.

4. If management cannot contact the withdrawing Builder or obtain their cooperation, it may decide how to proceed after making reasonable contact attempts over an appropriate period.

5. Registered Schematics are governed by Article 9. Withdrawal alone does not automatically terminate existing licenses.

6. Resource packs, models, textures, audio, and other data are governed by applicable license terms agreed with creators or rights holders and by related regulations.

7. When registering or providing resource packs or similar data, the scope of permission for use, modification, redistribution, and continued use after withdrawal must be clarified.

8. Participation in or withdrawal from MJX does not, by itself, authorize continued use of data without the rights holder’s permission.

If the rights holder requests that use cease, management must review existing license terms, third-party rights, and effects on installed facilities before responding.

9. Existing buildings and railway lines are not automatically removed solely because a Builder withdraws.

10. Management may take necessary measures for legitimate reasons, such as infringement of rights or serious violations of these Regulations.

Chapter 5 — Disciplinary Measures and Dispute Resolution

Article 19 — Disciplinary Measures and Procedures

1. Management may determine necessary disciplinary measures by considering the violation's nature and impact, intent or negligence, prior violations, progress toward improvement, and related circumstances.

2. Measures may include cautions, warnings, requests for improvement or equipment modification, recovery of items or coins, temporary suspension of permissions, or revocation of Builder qualifications.

3. Before imposing serious measures such as suspension of permissions or revocation of qualifications, management must generally notify the Builder of the alleged violation and give them an opportunity to respond.

4. In emergencies, such as protecting safety, preventing serious harm, or preserving evidence, management may impose temporary restrictions without providing an opportunity for explanation in advance. It must notify the affected person of the reasons as soon as reasonably possible and provide an opportunity to explain afterward.

5. For serious disciplinary measures, management must notify the person of the measure, its reasons, and the applicable provisions.

6. As a rule, a person subject to discipline may appeal to the designated contact point within 14 days after notification. If reasonable circumstances prevented a timely appeal, management may consider accepting a late appeal.

7. The disciplinary notice must specify how and when to appeal. An appellant must identify the measure challenged and the reasons for the appeal.

8. On receiving an appeal, management must review the grounds for the disciplinary measure, the facts, the appellant's explanation, and relevant regulations before deciding whether to uphold, amend, or revoke the measure.

9. Wherever possible, an appeal should be reviewed by someone who was not directly involved in the original decision. If staffing or other circumstances make that difficult, management should consider other ways to ensure impartiality.

10. Unfavorable treatment solely because a person has lodged an appeal is prohibited.

11. Filing an appeal does not automatically suspend a disciplinary measure. However, management may temporarily suspend, amend, or lift the measure when necessary.

12. Restrictions needed for emergency safety may remain in place during review, but their necessity must be reassessed as appropriate, and they must be lifted or changed when no longer necessary.

13. The appeals procedure in Article 12 of the General Server Regulations also applies. Where procedures or authority differ, the priority rules in Article 22 govern.

14. The implementation and recording of disciplinary measures, and final decisions, are governed by the General Server Regulations and other related rules.

Article 20 — Dispute Resolution Between Builders

1. If a dispute arises between Builders over land, buildings, railway lines, Schematics, work, or similar matters, the parties must first check the facts and make reasonable efforts to resolve it through discussion.

2. If the parties cannot resolve the dispute, they may request mediation or a decision from management.

3. Management must consider both parties' explanations, evidence, relevant regulations, and the history of the matter before making a decision.

4. In emergencies involving serious disruption, infringement of rights, or harm to the server, management may take necessary action without waiting for negotiations.

5. Appeals against management decisions are governed by Article 19 and the General Server Regulations.

Article 21 — Joint Development

1. When multiple Builders develop together, they should agree in advance, wherever possible, on their respective tasks, management duties, permitted uses of deliverables, and creator attribution.

2. Rights to jointly developed work are governed by the parties' agreements, each creator's rights, and applicable regulations.

3. The absence of an agreement does not, by itself, authorize anyone to freely use or modify another participant's work.

4. Disputes concerning rights or management are handled under Article 20.

5. Management may request development records or details of responsibility assignments when necessary.

Chapter 6 — Supplementary Provisions

Article 22 — Relationship with the Common Regulations and Amendment of These Rules

1. Matters not addressed by these Regulations are governed by the MJX General Server Regulations and other related rules.

2. In a conflict, the common regulations generally take precedence, unless they expressly permit special provisions in individual regulations.

3. MJX may make changes concerning MGSA's common systems only within the limits of MJX's own authority.

4. Management may revise these Regulations as necessary. As a general rule, changes and their effective dates must be announced in advance.

5. When existing Builders’ rights or activities are significantly affected, transitional measures may be established as necessary.

6. Detailed rules and operational standards may be established only insofar as they do not conflict with these Regulations or higher-level rules.

7. Questions of interpretation and contradictions between provisions must be decided in light of related regulations, the intent of these Regulations, and MJX’s operational purposes.

8. Final decisions concerning MJX as a whole are governed by the Supreme Administrator Regulations.

Supplementary Provisions

1. These Regulations took effect on November 1, 2026.

2. Existing Builder qualifications, priority holding rights, registered Schematics, buildings, and railway lines are generally not revoked solely because these Regulations take effect.

3. Management may establish a reasonable transition period for newly required applications, registrations, identification notices, or similar procedures.

4. If existing practices differ from these Regulations, management must inform the affected Builders of the changes and make individual adjustments when necessary.

5. For Schematics and resource packs registered before these Regulations take effect, existing agreements and rights must be checked, and permissions reconfirmed or registrations renewed when necessary. Taking effect does not, by itself, expand the scope of existing licenses.

6. Management may separately establish detailed rules, such as specific operational standards and lists of prohibited or permitted equipment.

Visitor Regulations

Effective date: November 1, 2026

Visitors are the general player category for people who enjoy sightseeing, shopping, transportation, work, daily life, events, and other activities in MJX’s cities.

1. Participation Environment

As a general rule, Visitors participate in Survival Mode.

However, under the protection mod adopted by MJX, placing or breaking blocks and modifying urban facilities is restricted during ordinary play.

In areas where management permits building or breaking blocks, Visitors may perform those actions within the permissions granted.

On joining MJX, Visitors receive an initial allowance from the MJX administration.

2. Ways to Earn Money

Visitors can earn coins or points through the following activities:

The type of reward—coins or points—depends on the activity.

• Customer service

• Driving

• Providing guidance

• Contracted work

• Running a shop

• Selling goods

• Operating vending machines

• Participating in events and competitions

• Other permitted jobs and services

3. Ways to Spend Money

• Transportation: MTR fares, commuter passes, and expressway tolls

• Mobility: Vehicles and dealerships; MTS / Kaminari-related products

• Equipment: TacZ weapons, ammunition, and other supported gear

Note: The use of weapons and ammunition is subject to MJX's combat and safety rules.

• Daily life: JNC furniture and appliances, food and beverages, shop goods, buying or renting property, and other eligible goods or services

• Converting points and coins: In some events, participants may be able to convert their points into coins.

MTR Japan Nexus — Economic Regulations

Established October 5, 2026

MTR Japan Nexus

Chapter 1 — General Provisions

Article 1 — Purpose

These Regulations establish the necessary rules for economic activity within MTR Japan Nexus (MJX), provide participants with opportunities to live, work, consume, and conduct businesses, and aim to support a healthy and sustainable economy.

Article 2 — Fundamental Principles

1. Economic activity within MJX is based on participants' freedom to offer goods, services, and labor.

2. MJX provides the necessary public services so participants can maintain a minimum standard of living and activity.

3. Public services aim to provide a stable supply of goods and services essential to participants; they do not aim to offer premium quality, speed, frequency, convenience, or other added value.

4. Private businesses may differentiate their offerings from public services through quality, speed, frequency, convenience, facilities, added value, and other features.

5. Administrators may establish or change prices, wages, and other economic conditions as needed to ensure the stability and continuity of MJX’s economy.

Article 3 — Definitions

The following terms have the meanings set out below in these Regulations:

1. "Administrator": A person with administrative authority in MJX.

2. “Supreme Administrator”: The person holding the highest administrative authority in MJX.

3. "Participant": Any person who participates in MJX.

4. “Visitor”: A person who participates in everyday urban life, economic activities, and other general activities within MJX.

5. "Builder": A participant whose primary activities are developing and constructing MJX cities, buildings, transportation facilities, or other infrastructure.

6. "Business Operator": A person who offers goods or services within MJX on an ongoing or repeated basis.

7. "Public Service": An activity operated by an administrator to provide MJX participants with essential goods or services.

8. "Private Business": A business run by an administrator or another participant on their own account that is not classified as a public service.

9. "Labor": An activity in which a person supplies work or working time under a business operator's instructions or supervision in exchange for wages.

10. “Construction Contract”: A contract under which a person receives remuneration to provide completed buildings, facilities, or other deliverables.

Chapter 2 — Builders and Visitors

Article 4 — Builder Activities

1. A Builder’s primary role is urban development and construction within MJX.

2. As a rule, the product-purchase, wage, housing, labor, and other economic restrictions in these Regulations do not apply to Builders’ ordinary urban-development and construction activities.

3. Builders may use Creative mode or other environments authorized by management to the extent needed for urban development.

4. As a general rule, construction materials, tools, and other resources needed for Builders' ordinary urban-development and construction activities are not subject to economic transactions.

Article 5 — Builder's Economic Activities

1. Even Builders are subject to these Regulations when they operate stores, sell goods, provide services, hire workers, or conduct other economic activities.

2. As a general rule, when a Builder provides construction skills or services to another participant in return for payment for a completed deliverable, the arrangement is treated as a construction contract.

3. As a rule, provisions on wages and working hours for ordinary employment do not apply to construction contracts.

4. Builder status alone does not exempt a participant from obligations applicable to their economic activities.

5. Notwithstanding the preceding paragraphs, where work under a construction contract in practice consists of hourly labor performed under a business operator's direction or supervision, it may be treated as ordinary labor.

Article 6 — Visitor Activities

1. Visitors may engage in labor, business, consumption, residence, and other economic activities within MJX.

2. These Regulations apply to Visitors' ordinary economic activities.

3. Visitors undertaking urban development or construction may obtain the necessary materials and goods through public services or private businesses.

Chapter 3 — Currency and Initial Funds

Article 7 — Currency

1. As a general rule, G is used for economic activities within MJX.

2. G is used as payment for goods, services, labor, and other economic activities within MJX.

3. The issuance and distribution of G and other activities affecting the money supply are managed by administrators as a rule.

Article 8 — Issuance and Management of G

1. Administrators may issue or distribute G for public services, rewards, compensation, initial allowances, or other MJX operational needs.

2. Administrators may adjust the amount of G issued as necessary to prevent inflation or other economic problems resulting from excessive issuance.

3. When administrators issue new G in quantities that significantly affect the economy, they must record the reasons and purposes.

Article 9 — Initial Allowance

1. As a general rule, new Visitors receive the following initial allowance:

• Bread: 1 stack (64 items); starting funds: 2,000G

2. Administrators may change the initial allowance in response to economic conditions or other circumstances.

3. Administrators may decide not to provide Builders with the normal initial allowance.

Chapter 4 — Public Services and Pricing

Article 10 — Public Services

1. Administrators may provide public services supplying goods and services necessary for participants’ minimum standard of living and activity within MJX.

2. Public services aim to ensure the minimum standard of living and activity.

3. Public services are not intended to maximize profits or surpass private businesses’ service levels.

4. Administrators determine the scope of public services as necessary.

5. Public services may provide basic or limited quality, speed, frequency, convenience, or other features, provided that they fulfill their essential purpose.

Article 11 — Public Prices

1. Administrators may set public prices for goods and services offered through public services.

2. A public price serves as the benchmark for maintaining the minimum required service level for the goods or services concerned within MJX.

3. Administrators may adjust public prices in response to economic conditions, delivery costs, usage patterns, or other circumstances.

4. As a rule, private operators must not offer goods or services for which a public price has been set at a price below that public price.

5. The preceding paragraph does not apply where an administrator has specifically authorized an exception.

Article 12 — Food Products

1. The public price for bread is 150G for six pieces.

2. Administrators may change the price in the preceding paragraph based on economic conditions or other circumstances.

3. Where management sets a public price for food items other than bread, the preceding article applies.

4. Businesses may freely price food products for which management has not set a public price.

Article 13 — Prices Charged by Private Businesses

1. Private operators may freely set prices for goods and services subject to public prices, provided the prices are at or above the public price.

2. In addition to price, private businesses may differentiate themselves from public services through quality, speed, frequency, convenience, facilities, location, added value, and other factors.

3. Even when private businesses offer goods or services equivalent to those provided publicly, they are not required to match the public service's quality, speed, frequency, or convenience.

Chapter 5 — Housing

Article 14 — Public housing

1. Administrators may provide public housing to ensure participants have access to a basic living environment.

2. Public housing rent is generally 10,000G per month.

3. Public housing facilities and services are based on the minimum requirements for living accommodation.

4. Public housing is not intended to offer luxury, enhanced comfort, or other premium features.

Article 15 — Acquisition of Public Housing

1. Management may introduce a program under which a public-housing tenant becomes eligible to acquire the home after paying rent continuously for 12 months.

2. If such a home-acquisition program is introduced, management must separately establish conditions concerning ownership, the associated land, renovations, transfers, sales, and other necessary matters.

3. Administrators may modify or suspend the acquisition scheme in response to economic conditions or changes to the housing system.

Article 16 — Privately Operated Housing

1. Private businesses may provide housing and other buildings.

2. Private businesses may set prices and contract terms for privately operated housing, provided they comply with MJX regulations.

3. Private housing operators may freely determine prices, floor areas, amenities, locations, and other features.

4. If a public price has been set for privately operated housing, Articles 11 and 13 apply.

Chapter 6 — Transportation

Article 17 — Public Transportation

1. Administrators may provide public transportation as a basic means for participants to travel within MJX.

2. Public transportation aims to ensure at least basic access to district offices and other major public facilities.

3. Public transportation is not necessarily intended to offer high speeds, frequent service, convenience, comfort, or other premium features.

4. Public transportation may operate simply or inefficiently as long as it fulfills its minimum objectives.

Article 18 — Public Transportation Fares

1. As a rule, the minimum fare for public transportation is 100G.

2. Administrators may set fares according to routes, distance, usage, operating conditions, and other relevant circumstances.

3. Administrators may change fares according to economic and transportation conditions.

Article 19 — Privately Operated Transportation

1. Private businesses may offer transportation services separately from public transportation.

2. Private transportation operators may set their fares subject to these Regulations and any other pricing requirements set by management.

3. Private transportation may distinguish itself from public transportation through speed, service frequency, convenience, comfort, equipment, service area, and other added value.

Chapter 7 — Construction Materials and Tools

Article 20 — Building Materials

1. Administrators may sell the basic construction materials required for building activities to Visitors and other participants who need them.

2. As a general rule, basic building materials are sold in stacks of 64 items.

3. Administrators determine sale prices in light of economic conditions and other relevant circumstances.

4. Builders do not need to purchase materials under this Article for their ordinary urban-development or construction activities.

Article 21 — Tools

1. When Visitors join MJX, administrators may provide basic tools needed for activities free of charge.

2. Higher-performance tools, specialized tools, and additional equipment may be offered for a fee.

3. Free provision of ordinary tools is not necessary for Builders using Creative mode or similar environments.

4. Administrators may change which tools are provided free of charge in light of game balance and economic conditions.

Chapter 8 — Wages and Labor

Article 22 — Basic Wage Rules

1. As a general rule, participants who perform labor within MJX are entitled to wages appropriate to the work performed.

2. Wages must be paid in G.

Article 23 — Construction Contracts

1. Builders and other participants may undertake paid contracts to provide completed buildings, facilities, or other deliverables.

2. Parties to a construction contract must agree in advance on payment, deliverables, deadlines, specifications, and other terms.

3. Construction contracts are, as a general rule, treated separately from ordinary wage employment.

4. The minimum wage in Article 24 and the maximum wage in Article 25 do not directly apply to remuneration for construction contracts.

5. If a construction contract in practice consists of hourly labor performed under a business operator's instructions or supervision, the work may be classified as ordinary labor.

Article 24 — Minimum Wage

As a rule, wages for work must not be less than 1,000G per hour.

Article 25 — Maximum Wage

1. As a rule, wages for work must not exceed 10,000G per hour.

2. The limit in the preceding paragraph is intended to prevent excessive currency inflow and economic instability within MJX.

3. If an administrator recognizes special circumstances, the maximum wage in paragraph 1 may be temporarily adjusted.

Article 26 — Wage Calculation

1. As a general rule, wages are calculated in 10-minute increments.

2. For work paid at 1,000G per hour, the reference amount is approximately 167G per 10 minutes.

3. Employers must specify the wage offered when recruiting, with amounts that can be calculated in 10-minute units.

Chapter 9 — Employment

Article 27 — Disclosure of Employment Conditions

When a business recruits workers, it must clearly state the following information in the job posting:

1. Terms of employment

2. Wages

3. Duties to be performed

4. Other conditions required

Article 28 — Number of Employees

1. As a general rule, a business may employ up to three workers simultaneously unless an administrator specifies otherwise.

2. Administrators may change the worker limit in the preceding paragraph in consideration of business scale, usage, public interest, and other circumstances.

3. Employers must not prevent workers from working for other employers.

4. Employers must not force workers to join their business or work exclusively for them.

Article 29 — Working Hours

1. As a rule, a single work session must last at least 10 minutes and no more than 3 hours.

2. If an administrator considers it necessary, workers may be employed beyond the limit in the preceding paragraph.

3. If a job requires a lengthy work session, the business operator must state this when advertising the position.

Article 30 — Payment of Wages

1. Employers must pay wages within three days after the work ends.

2. When a worker reports unpaid wages, an administrator may investigate the facts as necessary.

3. If unpaid wages are confirmed, an administrator may advance the unpaid amount on the employer’s behalf when necessary.

4. In the circumstances described in the preceding paragraph, the employer who failed to pay wages generally may not hire new workers for 30 days.

Article 31 — Employment Records

1. When hiring workers, employers must record the following details:

• Employer name • Worker name • Work description • Working hours • Wages

2. Employment records generally do not have to be made public.

3. If an administrator requests employment records, the employer must submit them within three days.

4. Administrators may generally restrict employers who fail to create employment records or record required details from hiring new workers for 14 days.

Chapter 10 — Business and Trading

Article 32 — Business Activities

1. Participants may operate businesses offering goods or services within MJX.

2. Businesses may determine their activities, services, and prices, provided they comply with MJX regulations.

3. Business operators may compete or cooperate with other businesses.

Article 33 — Relationship with Public Services

1. Public services are intended to provide the minimum essential level of service within MJX.

2. Public services are not required to match or exceed the quality, speed, frequency, or convenience offered by private businesses.

3. Private businesses may attract customers by offering higher quality, speed, frequency, convenience, or other added value than public services.

4. As long as a public service meets the minimum essential standard, it need not be improved solely because it is less efficient or convenient than a private alternative.

Article 34 — Transactions Between Individuals

1. Participants may trade goods, services, and G with one another.

2. The parties must agree on prices and terms for transactions between individuals.

3. False statements about a transaction, deliberate nonperformance, and other conduct intended to deceive the other party are prohibited.

Article 35 — Fairness of the Market

1. Participants must not improperly interfere with the legitimate economic activities of others.

2. Bulk buying of goods or services, deliberate interruptions of supply, price manipulation, and other acts that unfairly disrupt MJX’s economy are prohibited.

3. A monopoly held by a particular business or participant is not, in itself, automatically prohibited. However, management may take necessary measures against the use of a monopolistic position to unfairly obstruct others’ economic activities.

Chapter 11 — Business Activities of Administrators

Article 36 — Privately Operated Businesses Run by Administrators

1. Administrators may operate private businesses.

2. When an administrator operates a private business, these Regulations also apply to that business.

3. Being an administrator does not exempt a person from obligations concerning prices, wages, employment, business activities, or other obligations under these Regulations.

4. As a general rule, decisions under these Regulations concerning an administrator's own private business must be entrusted to another administrator or the Supreme Administrator.

5. Decisions concerning the Supreme Administrator's own private business must be reviewed by other administrators wherever possible.

Chapter 12 — Economic Management

Article 37 — Monitoring Economic Conditions

Administrators may review MJX’s money supply, prices, wages, business activities, and other economic circumstances as necessary.

Article 38 — Economic Adjustments

Administrators may change prices, wages, initial allowances, or other economic conditions in the following cases or whenever necessary to maintain MJX’s economy:

1. When prices rise or fall significantly

2. When the amount of currency in circulation becomes excessive

3. When particular goods or services are in severe shortage

4. When business activities can no longer operate viably

5. When MJX's game balance is significantly affected

Article 39 — Recovery of G

1. To maintain economic stability, administrators may recover G through sales of goods, service provision, taxes, or other appropriate means.

2. When recovering G, management must clarify the purpose and scope as far as possible.

Article 40 — Exceptions

Administrators may grant exceptions to parts of these Regulations after considering public interest, urgency, business size, and other circumstances.

Chapter 13 — Supreme Administrator

Article 41 — Powers of the Supreme Administrator

1. The Supreme Administrator holds the highest administrative authority in MJX.

2. Notwithstanding these Regulations or other MJX rules, the Supreme Administrator may make final decisions on matters necessary for MJX's economic management.

3. The Supreme Administrator may amend, suspend, exempt from application, or take other necessary measures concerning all or part of these Regulations, taking into account economic conditions, public interest, emergencies, and other circumstances.

4. When economically necessary, the Supreme Administrator may determine matters not specified in these Regulations concerning prices, wages, numbers of employees, initial allowances, public services, or other economic systems.

5. Decisions made by the Supreme Administrator under these Regulations are final within MJX.

Article 42 — Exercise of Authority by the Supreme Administrator

1. The authority granted to the Supreme Administrator under other MJX regulations is not restricted by these Regulations.

2. If these Regulations conflict with a decision of the Supreme Administrator, the Supreme Administrator's decision takes precedence.

3. The Supreme Administrator may direct amendments to these Regulations when necessary.

Article 43 — Private Business Activities of the Supreme Administrator

1. Article 36 also applies when the Supreme Administrator operates a private business.

2. When the Supreme Administrator operates a private business, they are treated as a business operator under these Regulations.

3. Notwithstanding the preceding two paragraphs, the Supreme Administrator may exercise the authority in Article 41 when necessary for MJX’s overall interests and economic stability.

Chapter 14 — Responses to Violations

Article 44 — Response to violations

1. MJX may take necessary measures under other regulations against business operators or participants who violate these Regulations.

2. Administrators may determine necessary measures after considering the severity and impact of the violation, intent or negligence, and other relevant circumstances.

3. Measures may include:

• Warnings • Restrictions on transactions or business activities • Restrictions on new hiring • Business suspension • Revocation of economic benefits • Other measures deemed necessary by administrators

4. Notwithstanding the preceding paragraphs, the Supreme Administrator may decide final measures necessary to protect MJX’s overall interests and order.

Article 45 — Fraudulent Transactions and Related Conduct

The following acts are prohibited:

1. Receiving G without intending to provide the promised goods or services

2. Entering into transactions by misrepresenting the terms

3. Failing to pay agreed wages without legitimate grounds

4. Illicitly duplicating or altering G or goods, or otherwise misusing the economic system

5. Other acts that seriously undermine the fairness of MJX’s economy

Chapter 15 — Amendments

Article 46 — Amendments

1. These Regulations may be amended in response to MJX's economic or operational circumstances.

2. As a general rule, amendments must be communicated to participants.

3. In the event of sudden economic changes or other emergencies, management may implement necessary measures first and amend these Regulations afterward.

Article 47 — Amendments by the Supreme Administrator

1. The Supreme Administrator may amend these Regulations or establish economic systems not otherwise specified in them when necessary.

2. If the measures in the preceding paragraph are taken, their contents must be communicated to participants to the extent possible.

End of Regulations

Effective date: November 1, 2026

MTR Japan Nexus — Resource Pack Standardization Rules

Revision approved: November 1, 2026 / Effective date: November 1, 2026

Operator: MTR Japan Nexus (MJX)

Preamble

These Regulations establish rules for proper management and use of official resource packs within MTR Japan Nexus (MJX) and for protecting their creators’ rights.

MJX respects participants' creative work and its results, and strives to maintain an environment in which resource packs can be used safely and continuously.

These Rules apply alongside the MJX General Server Regulations and other related rules.

Article 1 — Scope and Definitions

1. These Rules apply to resource packs officially managed and distributed by MJX management, including their models, textures, audio, configuration files, and other component data.

2. The roles and terms used in these Rules are defined as follows:

◦ Administrator: A person responsible for servers, resource packs, or other operations under MJX regulations and assigned permissions.

◦ Builder: A person authorized to perform urban development, construction, and railway or transport infrastructure work through MGSA’s selection process or procedures recognized by MJX.

◦ Visitor: A general participant in MJX who belongs to the participation category defined in the relevant MJX regulations.

◦ Creator: A person who created models, textures, sounds, or other data included in a resource pack.

◦ Rights holder: A person who holds copyright or other authority to grant permission to use the data.

3. In these Rules, an "official resource pack" means a resource pack provided through a method designated by MJX management.

4. As a general rule, permission granted under these Rules is limited to use within MJX's Minecraft server.

5. Resource packs formerly used by MTR Japan Server (MJaS) do not automatically become MJX official resource packs under these Rules.

Article 2 — Permitted Uses

1. MJX participants may use the official resource pack according to their role and the rights holders’ terms of use.

2. Builders may use authorized resource packs for urban development, railway construction, transportation infrastructure, and other permitted activities within MJX.

3. Visitors may use the official resource pack to display MJX vehicles, buildings, equipment, and other objects correctly and to participate in permitted in-game activities.

4. Normal downloading and loading by the Minecraft client, and creation of necessary caches, are permitted under these Regulations. This does not permit free redistribution or modification.

5. Users must also comply with any additional terms of use applicable to specific vehicles, models, or other assets.

6. Publishing screenshots or videos made using the resource pack is subject to rules separately established by MJX and the rights holders’ terms of use.

7. This Article does not authorize use of third-party copyrighted works beyond the rights holder's permission.

Article 3 — Prohibitions

3-1 Common Prohibitions

The following actions are prohibited for all users:

1. Redistributing all or part of the official resource pack to third parties without the necessary authority or permission.

2. Using official resource packs without permission on Minecraft servers other than MJX, in single-player worlds, or in any other external environment.

3. Extracting, modifying, porting, or repurposing vehicles, models, textures, sounds, or other data from the resource pack without permission.

4. Obtaining or publishing official resource packs without authorization through methods other than those officially designated by management.

5. Improperly removing or altering creator credits, rights notices, or terms of use.

6. Using resource packs to harass, discriminate against, threaten, or otherwise disturb other users.

7. Using resource packs for political or religious promotion or other purposes contrary to the MJX General Server Regulations.

8. Conduct that violates laws, public order or morals, the MJX General Server Regulations, or other related rules.

9. Any other conduct that MJX management deems inappropriate on specific grounds.

Exceptions based on valid permission from the rights holder or applicable law are recognized only within the scope of that permission or law.

3-2 Prohibited Conduct by Builders

In addition to the preceding paragraph, Builders must not engage in the following acts:

1. Installing vehicles or models made by other creators on one’s own railway lines or facilities in violation of their permitted terms of use.

2. Modifying, re-registering, or redistributing another creator's work without the necessary permission.

3. Selling, commercializing, or otherwise commercially exploiting data from the resource pack without the necessary permission from the rights holder.

4. Using a vehicle in accident depictions without permission from that vehicle’s original creator.

5. Misrepresenting oneself as the creator or legitimate rights holder of someone else’s work.

3-3 Depictions of Vehicle Accidents

1. As a general rule, depicting an accident involving a vehicle included in an official resource pack requires the vehicle creator's prior permission.

2. A vehicle must not be used in accident depictions if its creator has prohibited such use.

3. If the creator's permission cannot be verified, the use must be treated as unauthorized.

4. MJX management's approval of a plan or event does not replace the vehicle creator's permission.

5. For jointly created works or other assets involving multiple rights holders, permission must be obtained from all relevant rights holders as required.

6. Even with the creator's permission, the user must comply with the MJX General Server Regulations and other related rules.

3-4 Modifications and Permissions

1. As a general rule, modifying vehicles, models, or other assets created by others requires the rights holder's permission.

2. If a work specifies whether modification is allowed and under what conditions, those conditions must be followed.

3. Permission should preferably be documented in a form the parties can verify, specifying the data covered, purpose of use, proposed modifications, and whether redistribution is allowed.

4. Permission to modify a work does not, by itself, authorize redistribution, external use, commercial secondary use of the data, or other activities.

Article 4 — Protection of Creators and Rights Holders

1. Providing data for inclusion in an official resource pack does not, by itself, transfer the creator's or rights holder's copyright or other rights to MJX management.

2. When works are registered or included, the necessary permission of the rights holders and the applicable terms of use must be confirmed.

3. MJX management's approval of registration or inclusion does not transfer the rights holder's rights or authorize uses beyond the granted permission.

4. Creators may specify conditions for use, modification, and reuse by other Builders within the scope of the rights they are entitled to license.

5. For jointly created works, the agreements among relevant rights holders and any third-party rights must be respected.

6. If a rights holder requests that use stop, that registration be withdrawn, or that other action be taken, MJX management must review existing license terms, actual usage, and third-party rights before responding.

7. Deregistration or withdrawal from MJX does not, by itself, extinguish copyrights or other rights.

8. This Article does not override the treatment of creative works or registered data under the MJX Builder Regulations or other related rules.

Article 5 — Distribution and Suspension of Distribution

1. As a rule, distribution and updates of the official resource pack are carried out by MJX management or persons properly authorized to do so.

2. MJX management may temporarily suspend distribution, updates, or use of resource packs in the following circumstances:

◦ If serious deficiencies or defects are found in the data.

◦ When copyright or other rights-related problems are confirmed.

◦ If unauthorized use, unauthorized redistribution, or another rules violation is confirmed.

◦ If security issues arise or server operations are seriously disrupted.

◦ When suspension is deemed necessary for other reasonable grounds.

3. If distribution is suspended, management will publicize the reasons, effects, and anticipated resumption to the extent possible.

4. In an emergency, management may suspend distribution without prior notice. An explanation will be provided afterward when necessary.

5. Suspension of distribution does not extinguish the rights held by rights holders.

Article 6 — Response to violations

1. If a violation of these Rules is confirmed, MJX management may take necessary measures after considering the violation's nature and impact, intent or negligence, and any prior violations.

2. Measures may include the following:

◦ Caution or warning.

◦ Suspension of use of the relevant data.

◦ Restrictions on use of the resource pack.

◦ Temporary suspension of related activity permissions.

◦ Restrictions on access to the Minecraft server or other MJX-related services.

◦ Suspension of server access or a ban in serious cases.

3. Where unauthorized redistribution, rights infringement, or similar conduct is suspected, MJX management may work with the rights holder to submit takedown or suspension requests, or other appropriate notices, to the relevant service operators.

4. Requests under the preceding paragraph must be based on appropriate rights or legitimate grounds. They do not imply that MJX management has authority to directly shut down or delete external services.

5. Violations or disciplinary measures involving MGSA are governed by Article 7 of the MJX General Server Regulations and other applicable rules.

6. As a general rule, management must notify the person concerned of the reasons for disciplinary action. Temporary measures may be taken in advance where necessary for urgent safety or evidence-preservation purposes.

Article 7 — Appeals

1. Users may request an explanation of disciplinary measures imposed under these Rules and appeal to MJX management.

2. The filing and review of appeals are governed by Article 12 of the MJX General Server Regulations and other related rules.

3. Where a disciplinary measure against a Builder is subject to Article 19 of the Builder Regulations, the procedures under that article also apply.

4. After reviewing an appeal, management may amend, reduce, or lift the disciplinary measure if it considers that necessary.

5. Users must not be subjected to unfavorable measures solely because they filed an appeal.

6. The effectiveness of measures during an appeal is governed by related regulations. Management may suspend, modify, or lift a measure when necessary.

7. Measures that MJX cannot change independently, such as MGSA-wide bans, are governed by the relevant procedures and authorities.

Article 8 — Disclaimer

1. MJX management does not guarantee that official resource packs will function correctly in every environment or remain continuously available.

2. Distribution, updates, or use of resource packs may be temporarily restricted due to system failures, specification changes, communication outages, or other circumstances.

3. Users must comply with these Rules and related regulations when using resource packs.

4. MJX management’s responsibilities are governed by applicable laws. These Regulations do not exempt any liability that cannot legally be excluded or limited.

Article 9 — Information handling

1. The privacy policy currently in effect within MJX applies to user information collected in connection with resource-pack distribution, authentication, misuse prevention, or maintenance.

2. The types of information collected, purposes of use, retention periods, and sharing with third parties are governed by the Privacy Policy.

3. These Rules do not independently authorize collection or use of personal information not covered by the Privacy Policy.

Article 10 — Related Regulations and Amendments

1. Matters not specified in these Rules are governed by the MJX General Server Regulations, MJX Builder Regulations, and other applicable rules.

2. Where interpretations of these Regulations and other rules differ, decisions must take account of the activities concerned, the rights involved, and each rule’s scope, in accordance with the MJX General Server Regulations and other related rules.

3. Under no circumstances do these Regulations automatically extinguish rights granted by law or rights held by legitimate rights holders.

4. MJX management may revise these Regulations in response to changes in the system, operating environment, rights, laws, or other relevant circumstances.

5. As a rule, the contents and effective dates of revisions will be announced in advance. In emergencies, necessary measures may be taken first and announced as soon as possible afterward.

6. Revising these Rules does not, by itself, expand the scope of existing permissions granted by creators or rights holders.

Supplementary Provisions

1. These Regulations took effect on November 1, 2026.

2. These Rules apply to the resource packs currently officially managed and distributed by MJX.

MJX Cinema System Usage Policy

MTR Japan Nexus (MJX)

Article 1 — Purpose

This policy establishes the rules for safe and appropriate use of the cinema system on the MJX server.

This system may handle video and audio content associated with external services, including YouTube.

Article 2 — Content Permitted for Use

As a general rule, users may use the following types of content:

1. Video and audio created by the user for which the user holds the necessary rights

2. Content for which the necessary permission has been obtained from the rights holder

3. Content that may be screened or distributed within the scope of its terms of use

4. Content whose use has been approved by MJX management

Note: Public availability on YouTube does not mean that redistribution or screening within MJX is permitted.

Article 3 — Prohibitions

The following acts are prohibited:

1. Screening or redistributing movies, anime, paid streaming works, or similar content without the copyright holder’s permission

2. Use of illegally uploaded content

3. Circumventing access restrictions on paid services or copy-protection measures

4. Playing video or audio for the purpose of harassing or disturbing other users

5. Actions that place an excessive load on the system

6. Any other conduct that violates applicable laws, the terms of external services, or the MJX General Server Regulations

Article 4 — Handling of YouTube Content

When using YouTube content, comply with YouTube’s terms of service and the terms of use set by the rights holders.

Even if this system can technically play content, that does not necessarily mean its use is permitted.

Users must verify that they have the necessary rights and permissions for each act of obtaining, storing, screening, or redistributing external content.

Article 5 — Operation by Administrators

MJX management may use administrative functions unavailable to ordinary users for system maintenance, operational testing, content management, and event administration.

Administrators must also comply with applicable laws, copyright requirements, and external services' terms when handling content.

Article 6 — Response to violations

If a violation of this policy is confirmed, MJX management may take the following measures:

• Stop playback of or delete the relevant content

• Restrictions on use of the cinema system

• Suspend privileges within the server

• Suspension of access to the server in serious cases

Article 7 — Disclaimer

Please note that video or audio may fail to play properly due to changes to external services, network failures, system malfunctions, or similar circumstances.

This policy does not exclude or limit any liability that cannot legally be excluded or limited.

Article 8 — Policy Revisions

This policy may be revised in response to changes to system functions, laws, or the terms of external services.

MTR Japan Nexus — Privacy Policy

MTR Japan Nexus (MJX)

Article 0 — Purpose and Scope

1. MTR Japan Nexus (MJX) may collect and store information about users in providing and managing its Minecraft server and related services.

2. This Policy sets out the purposes, retention periods, management practices, and user rights relating to information collected by MJX, with the aim of ensuring its proper protection.

3. This policy applies to handling information described in this policy in connection with MJX-operated Minecraft servers, resource-pack management systems, and related services.

4. Before collecting additional information not covered by this Policy through a related service, MJX will disclose the categories of information, purposes of use, and other necessary details, and revise this Policy or provide a separate notice.

Article 1 — Information Collected

1-1 Information Collected with User Consent

MJX may collect and retain the following information after explaining the relevant feature and obtaining the user's consent.

• Minecraft account UUID

Users who do not consent to collection of this information may be unable to use certain features that require it.

1-2 Information Collected for Service Operations

When a user accesses the resource-pack management system, MJX may collect and retain the following information as necessary for its operation, maintenance, and security, regardless of whether the user has consented.

• Date and time of access

• Version of the resource-pack management Mod used

This information will be handled only for the purposes specified in this Policy.

1-3 Principles for Acquisition of Information

1. MJX collects information only to the extent needed for the stated purposes.

2. MJX will not collect information through deception or other improper means.

3. If MJX collects information other than that listed in this Article, it will provide appropriate notice of the information collected and its purposes of use.

Article 2 — Purpose of Use of Information

MJX uses the acquired information for the following purposes:

1. Preventing unauthorized use and redistribution of resource packs

2. Managing access logs and investigating unauthorized access

3. Investigation and troubleshooting of system failures, defects, and related problems

4. Improving resource packs and related systems

5. Analyzing usage and ensuring stable service provision

6. Security measures and maintaining the safety of the Services

7. Other administrative activities reasonably related to the purposes above

As a rule, MJX will obtain the person’s prior consent before handling personal information beyond what is necessary for the stated purposes, except where required or otherwise permitted by law.

Article 3 — Information Retention Period and Deletion

1. As a general rule, MJX retains collected user information until six months have elapsed after the user leaves or withdraws from MJX.

2. When the period in the preceding paragraph has elapsed, MJX will promptly delete the relevant user information.

3. If information is no longer needed to fulfill its purposes during the retention period, MJX will endeavor to delete it at an appropriate time.

4. Where laws require retention or other legal circumstances apply, MJX will handle the information in accordance with the law and only to the extent necessary.

5. When deleting information, MJX identifies the relevant records and removes them using appropriate methods.

Article 4 — Provision of Information to Third Parties

1. As a rule, MJX does not provide collected personal data to third parties without the data subject’s consent.

2. Notwithstanding the preceding paragraph, data may be provided in the following cases, to the extent permitted by law.

◦ Where the data subject has consented

◦ Where disclosure is based on applicable law

◦ Where necessary to protect a person's life, physical well-being, or property and obtaining their consent is difficult

◦ Where disclosure without consent is otherwise permitted under the Act on the Protection of Personal Information or other applicable laws

3. Even when responding to misuse, violations, or other problems, MJX will not disclose personal data to third parties unconditionally on that basis alone.

4. When sharing information with MGSA or other external organizations, MJX will first verify the individual's consent or another lawful basis for sharing.

5. When outsourcing personal-data processing, MJX will provide necessary and appropriate supervision of the contractor in accordance with the law.

Article 5 — Cookies and similar technologies

1. MJX's web-based systems may use cookies or similar technologies to maintain login sessions and provide other necessary functionality.

2. MJX will manage cookies and similar technologies appropriately and only to the extent necessary.

3. If cookies or similar technologies are used to collect additional information, MJX will provide any necessary notice according to the data collected and its purposes of use.

Article 6 — Access, Correction, and Deletion of Information by Users

1. Users may request disclosure, correction, suspension of use, deletion, or similar measures concerning information about themselves held by MJX.

2. Upon receiving a request, MJX will verify the requester's identity where necessary and respond in accordance with applicable laws and the purposes of use.

3. Identity verification generally uses only the minimum information necessary.

4. If MJX cannot fulfill a request, in whole or in part, including for legal reasons, it will explain the reasons to the extent possible.

5. Users may contact MJX management with questions about the handling of their information.

Article 7 — Information Security Management

1. MJX implements necessary and appropriate safeguards to prevent unauthorized access to, leakage, loss, or alteration of collected information.

2. As a general rule, access to user information is restricted to personnel whose authority requires it for MJX operations.

3. Administrators who handle information must not disclose information learned through their duties to external parties without legitimate grounds.

4. If information is leaked or another incident occurs, MJX will take necessary measures to contain the damage, investigate the cause, and prevent recurrence.

5. MJX will comply with any applicable legal obligations to report incidents or notify affected parties.
